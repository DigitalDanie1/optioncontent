Hypercall vaults: putting strategies at the heart of the exchange

NOVEMBER 6, 2026 Hypercall 6 min read

Vaults are a key product in Hypercall’s next chapter. They bring a new way to participate in its markets: choose a strategy and put capital behind it, without managing every trade yourself.

Pick the exposure, not every strike and expiry. A vault brings position sizing, collateral management and trading decisions inside a managed strategy. You choose the book you want to participate in. The manager runs it.

This is a product launch with a bigger ambition. Alongside Hypercall’s existing USDC lending pool, managed vaults can bring options expertise to users who want the strategy without running the book. Financing traders, providing liquidity and earning option premium are different opportunities. Vaults give those opportunities a clearer product form, and this is only the beginning for managed vaults on Hypercall.

One platform. Different ways to participate.

The opportunity is to bring more participants into the same trading ecosystem. Lending supplies financing to portfolio-margin traders. SLP is designed to let depositors participate in options market making. Covered-call strategies offer another way to put an underlying asset to work. Each product gives capital a distinct role.

Each strategy has its own earning engine. Understanding it lets you choose deliberately, rather than treating every yield headline as the same opportunity.

Two ways to put USDC to work

Hypercall’s existing USDC lending pool and SLP do different jobs. The lending pool finances portfolio-margin accounts. SLP is designed to run an options market-making book. One earns interest on loans; the other takes trading risk to earn spread and other strategy returns.

USDC lending: power the trading engine

Portfolio-margin traders hold collateral on HyperCore, but pay option premiums and expiry settlements in USDC on Hypercall. If their Hypercall cash is short, the pool can finance the gap automatically, subject to margin and liquidity checks. Selling an option does not itself draw a loan. Cash reaching the borrower’s Hypercall account repays debt before any surplus is swept back to HyperCore.

For lenders, depositing USDC mints pool shares. Interest increases the pool’s net asset value and share price rather than arriving as a separate payout. Idle cash earns no interest. The reserve factor directs 15% of borrower interest to a separate first-loss reserve; lenders receive the remaining share through pool value.

Why utilization changes the rate

Utilization is borrowed USDC divided by available cash plus loans. The production pool’s published rate model starts at 0% borrow APR, reaches 4% at 60% utilization, then rises steeply to 100% at full utilization. That steep section is a liquidity signal, designed to encourage repayment and attract cash when the pool is tight.

With utilization u written as a decimal, borrower APR is 0.04 × u / 0.60 up to the kink. Above it, borrower APR is 0.04 + 0.96 × (u - 0.60) / 0.40. Lender APR is borrower APR × utilization × (1 - 0.15).

These are annualized rate snapshots under the production model observed November 5, 2026, not promised returns. They are APR, not APY. Borrowing interest compounds continuously on a 365-day year, and rates change as utilization changes; multiplying a quoted APR by a year does not describe a guaranteed realized return.

At 100% utilization, no unborrowed cash remains in this simplified scenario. A high displayed rate can come with delayed withdrawals, not easy income. Lending withdrawals run through hourly epochs with one epoch of notice, then pay first in, first out as cash allows. The one-to-two-hour eligibility window is not a guaranteed payout time. Queued shares continue earning interest and bearing losses until paid.

Lending is not risk-free. Recoveries from borrower collateral and a finite first-loss reserve stand ahead of lenders, but any remaining loan loss reduces the share price. The reserve also has settlement obligations ahead of covering written-off loans. Smart-contract, engine, liquidity and USDC risks remain. A reserve factor is a funding rule, not a guarantee that losses are covered.

SLP: participate in the market-making engine

SLP takes the opportunity a step further: USDC for shares in a book designed to quote Hypercall options and hedge on Hyperliquid. Instead of placing every quote yourself, you can participate in the strategy behind the liquidity. Its core earning engine is the bid-ask spread: getting paid to provide the other side of a trade. Capturing that spread takes good pricing and hedging. Informed flow, hedge costs and large market moves can still turn a profitable-looking spread into a loss.

The earlier SLP announcement describes a basis hedge as a way to manage delta: long spot, short a perp, then adjust the short as the options exposure changes. The remaining short can collect funding when funding is favorable. That is a source of variable return, not a fixed coupon; it does not eliminate options tail risk.

SLP is also intended to compete with outside makers for the announced 1 million SYN monthly maker program. Rewards are based on eligible premium flow won, not simply time spent quoting. Related-account and self-trades are excluded, and rewards are subject to review. The pool is shared across qualifying makers, not a fixed SLP yield or a promised allocation to each depositor.

The prior blog also describes potential liquidation-auction participation and ways to deploy idle cash. Box-spread financing was explicitly described as coming soon, and parts of spot-in-vault support were still being built. Those are possibilities to follow as they ship, not assumptions inside a return estimate.

Built for strategies, not just deposits

The managed-vault design gives this product category a foundation to grow on: engine-based valuation, defined trading authority and a clear separation from withdrawal authority. The published design also describes mandate checks, high-water-mark performance fees and a process for paying withdrawal queues. These are meaningful building blocks for managed strategies on an exchange, while strategy and operational risks remain.

Each product pairs its strategy with its own fees, caps, settlement cadence and withdrawal terms. The USDC pool’s hourly withdrawal epochs apply to lending, not automatically to SLP. SLP’s final published terms will define its deposit and withdrawal experience.

Covered calls are a separate exposure

A covered-call strategy owns an asset and sells calls against it. The premium is the payment for giving up upside above the strike. If the asset falls, the premium can soften the loss, but it does not remove the downside. If it rallies through the strike, simply holding the asset can do better. A single-stock vault also brings concentrated exposure and tokenized-stock issuer risk.

Only the beginning for managed vaults

USDC lending supports the cash needs of active traders. SLP aims to turn market-making expertise into a strategy depositors can participate in. Covered calls bring a different trade-off: option premium in exchange for capped upside, while retaining the asset’s downside. Together, these products show why a strategy platform can be more useful than a single yield product.

A bigger opportunity, with a clear choice

The bigger vision is a home for managed strategies on Hypercall: a place where users choose the exposure and managers focus on execution. SLP and the wider vault lineup are a starting point for that vision. As new strategies are developed and validated, the opportunity is to turn more of the exchange’s trading capabilities into products users can participate in.

Before depositing, check each product’s mandate, manager, fees, limits and withdrawal terms. Backtested results, where shown, are historical models, not live returns or a promise of what comes next.

Hypercall’s markets are the foundation. Vaults make the strategies around those markets a product in their own right. That is what makes this launch exciting: more ways for capital to participate, a larger role for strategy managers and the beginning of a broader managed-vaults offering. Choose the strategy. Put capital behind the opportunity. This is only the beginning.
