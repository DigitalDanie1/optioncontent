How Hypercall Handles the SNDK 8-for-1 Split

NOVEMBER 17, 2026 Hypercall 3 min read

SanDisk splits 8-for-1 after the close on Thursday, November 19. Hypercall prices SNDK options off the xyz:SNDK market on Hyperliquid, and trade.xyz is settling and reopening that market around the split. We are adjusting SNDK options the way a listed options exchange would. The value of your position does not change.

The short version

  • SNDK options stop trading at 21:00 UTC on November 19 (4:00 PM New York, the closing auction).
  • The November 19 expiry settles normally. Its settlement window closes at the same moment.
  • Every later SNDK contract converts. One contract becomes eight, the strike is divided by eight, and your entry price is divided by eight.
  • Resting SNDK orders and open SNDK RFQs are cancelled. Re-enter them after trading resumes.
  • Trading resumes on November 20 once xyz:SNDK is back on external pricing, after the 9:30 AM New York open. We will post the moment it is live.

What happens to your position

Take a long December 25 $800 call on SNDK, 1 contract, bought at $40.

The math is the same identity every split uses. A call at strike K on a stock at S pays max(S − K, 0). After an 8-for-1 split the stock trades at S/8, and eight calls at K/8 pay 8 × max(S/8 − K/8, 0), which is the same number. Puts work the same way. No cash moves and no P&L is realized by the conversion.

Short positions convert the same way: short 1 becomes short 8, and the premium you collected is unchanged.

Strikes that do not divide evenly

Most SNDK strikes divide cleanly. Some do not: a $805 strike becomes $100.625. Converted contracts keep the exact strike, so nothing is rounded against you. New listings after the split use the normal strike grid around the new price. Where a converted strike lands exactly on a grid strike, they are the same contract.

During the pause

From the November 19 close until we resume:

  • No SNDK orders, RFQs or xyz:SNDK perp orders are accepted through Hypercall.
  • SNDK is marked at the November 19 close. We do not take prices from the closed market or from trade.xyz’s internal reopening price, and nothing from the pause feeds into settlement.
  • Margin and liquidation rules still apply to your account as normal.
  • No new SNDK expiries or strikes are listed.

If you hold xyz:SNDK perps, trade.xyz settles them at the Nasdaq closing price and cancels all orders, including take-profit and stop-loss. That is their process, not ours. See their notice.

Resuming

Before we turn trading back on, we run the conversion against every account with SNDK exposure and check margin at the live post-split price. We resume only once xyz:SNDK is on external pricing and the live price agrees with the close divided by eight. The November 20 expiry then settles on the post-split price at 21:00 UTC as usual.

After the split, SNDK is just SNDK at its new price. There is no lingering multiplier on spot, settlement, volatility surfaces or new listings.

Your trade history

Past trades keep their original symbols and strikes. A December $800 call you bought last month still shows as an $800 call at the price you paid, not as eight $100 calls. Your open position shows the converted contract. Charts and history are not split-adjusted.

Market makers

Resting quotes on the old instruments are cancelled at the conversion. Quote the new symbols from /markets once trading resumes. API details are in the changelog.
